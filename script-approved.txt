An engineer was tasked with building a skyscraper in an area frequently hit by strong winds and earthquakes.

He started by connecting massive steel columns to form a structural framework, then built the skyscraper one floor at a time.

But once the building was completed, powerful winds caused the upper floors to sway back and forth, shaking everything inside.

He immediately added diagonal steel braces to make the structure more rigid. The building became more stable, but it still swayed enough to make people inside uncomfortable.

That's when he came up with an unusual solution.

He installed a massive steel sphere near the top of the skyscraper, suspended it from heavy-duty steel cables, and connected it to a series of dampers.

When the wind pushed the building to the left, inertia caused the sphere to resist the movement, making it move in the opposite direction relative to the building.

As the building changed direction, the sphere continued swinging out of sync, generating a force that counteracted some of the building's motion.

The dampers absorbed the energy from these oscillations and dissipated it as heat.

As a result, the building's movements became less pronounced.

When another powerful windstorm hit, the massive steel sphere began swinging back and forth, while the people inside could continue working more comfortably.

It turns out that the secret to keeping a skyscraper stable isn't always making it more rigid...

It's knowing how to control the way it moves.
